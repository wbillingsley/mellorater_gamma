PAPER 1: THE 2020 FIVE DOMAINS MODEL — INCLUDING HUMAN–ANIMAL INTERACTIONS IN
ASSESSMENTS OF ANIMAL WELFARE
================================================================================

TWO CATEGORIES OF AFFECT

The first category, survival-critical negative affects, refers to experiences generated by
sensory inputs that register imbalances or disruptions in the internal physical/functional
state of animals. They include breathlessness, thirst, hunger, pain (~30 varieties),
nausea, dizziness, debility, weakness and sickness. These affects are designated as
survival-critical because they are aligned with essential components of genetically
embedded mechanisms that elicit or are associated with behaviours on which the survival
of the animals depends.

The undoubted negativity of each affect creates a sense of urgency, or a dominating
compulsion, to engage in behaviours which are specific to that affect and its resolution.
Once the behaviour achieves the required corrective physical/functional outcome, the
intensity of the negative affect declines. Unpleasant experiences that cannot be
effectively relieved through behavioural and physiological responses may have a greater
detrimental impact on the welfare state than acute but short-lived experiences.

The second category, situation-related negative affects, refers to experiences generated
by brain processing of sensory inputs that mainly originate from outside the body and
reflect the animal's perception of its external circumstances. These affects currently
include frustration, anger, helplessness, loneliness, boredom, depression, anxiety, fear,
panic and hypervigilance. Animals in impoverished and/or threatening situations may
experience these affects in various combinations.

The two categories are not mutually exclusive. Good or acceptable animal welfare cannot
be achieved simply by mitigating or avoiding negative experiences — some pleasurable
experiences are needed as well. Animals may have pleasurable experiences when their
external circumstances include: variability that provides an optimal balance between
predictability and novelty; species-specific needs for movement and exercise; access to
preferred sites for resting and thermal comfort; environmental choices that encourage
exploratory and foraging behaviours; availability of a variety of feeds; and circumstances
that enable social species to engage in bonding activities with familiar conspecifics,
including allogrooming, play behaviour, and other affiliative interactions. The associated
welfare-enhancing affects likely include comfort, pleasure, interest, attachment, confidence,
and a sense of being in control.


DOMAIN 4: BEHAVIOURAL INTERACTIONS

Domain 4 is intended to capture behavioural outputs as indices of animals' perceptions
of their external circumstances. It highlights the flexible agency-related behaviours
animals mount in response to variable, often unpredictable external events and conditions.

Agency indicates the intrinsic propensity of an animal to actively engage with its
physical, biological and social environment, beyond the degree demanded by its momentary
needs, in order to gather knowledge and enhance its skills for future use. The primary
focus of Domain 4 is on behavioural evidence of hindered and/or enhanced expression
of agency.

PARTICULAR NEGATIVE AFFECTS arise when agency-related behaviours are absent or
diminished. Examples include:
- The daily thwarting of normal long-duration grazing motivation in stabled horses
  fed with highly concentrated feeds which nevertheless meet their nutritional requirements
- The frustration of social species such as horses and elephants that are prevented from
  joining conspecifics engaged in social behaviours
- The yearning for company (loneliness) of isolated individuals of social species

POSITIVE AFFECTS arise in circumstances that provide opportunities which enhance
animals' ability to express agency-related behaviours, linked to affectively positive
experiences of anticipation, goal achievement and memory of success.

INTERACTIONS WITH THE ENVIRONMENT:
Negative (agency impeded): invariant, barren, confined environment → boredom,
helplessness, depression, withdrawal; choices markedly restricted → frustration;
foraging drive impeded.

Positive (agency promoted): varied, novel environment → interested, pleasantly
occupied; available engaging choices → calm, in control; free movement → engaged;
exploration and foraging → energised, focussed.

INTERACTIONS WITH OTHER ANIMALS:
Negative: animal-to-animal interactive activity constrained → loneliness, depression,
yearning for company; thwarted desire to play → frustration; significant threats with
limits on avoidance or escape → anger, anxiety, fear, panic, insecurity.

Positive: bonding/reaffirming bonds → affectionate sociability; playing →
excitation/playfulness; absence of threats, use of refuges → secure, protected, confident;
sufficient sleep/rest → energised, refreshed.

INTERACTIONS WITH HUMANS:
Negative human attributes → animal behaviours and affects: long flight distance,
hypervigilant, hyper-reactive, escape avoidance, freezing, cowering, withdrawn,
non-compliant; affects include anxiety, fear, panic, helplessness, pain, negative
cognitive bias.

Positive human attributes → animal behaviours and affects: short flight distance,
calm alertness, at ease with contact, compliantly responsive, seeks contact; affects
include calm, confident, feels in control, finds being bonded with humans rewarding.

Human–animal interactions likely to generate NEGATIVE affects:
- Persons whose current actions are directly unpleasant, threatening and/or noxious
  (e.g., aversive training methods; riders whipping tired horses in sport)
- Persons whose prior actions are remembered as aversive or noxious
- Bonded humans whose actions cause unintended harm (e.g., owners absent for long
  periods; owners delaying efficacious therapies; delayed end-of-life decisions)

Human–animal interactions likely to generate POSITIVE affects:
- The companionable presence of persons who provide company and feelings of safety
- Persons who provide preferred foods, tactile contacts and/or training reinforcements
- Persons participating in enjoyable routine and engagingly variable activities
- The calming presence of familiar persons in threatening circumstances
- Persons acting to end periods of deprivation, inhibition or harm


DOMAIN 5: MENTAL STATE

The fifth domain captures the overall mental experience of the animals, evaluated in
terms of the subjective experiences associated with conditions in the first four domains.
Welfare assessment must always be grounded in observable evidence from Domains 1 to 4
before making inferences about an animal's mental state.

PAPER 2: INTRODUCING THE MELLORATER — THE FIVE DOMAINS MODEL IN A WELFARE
MONITORING APP FOR ANIMAL GUARDIANS
================================================================================

HOW THE MELLORATER WORKS

The Mellorater uses a checklist of 18 statements based on the 2020 Five Domains Model.
It asks guardians to consider their level of agreement with each statement using a
five-point Likert scale (strongly disagree to strongly agree). Users can save notes and
upload images for future reference.

Users are also prompted to log their level of confidence in the evidence they used to
determine their response to each statement. The intention is to prompt the user to
question the reliability and quality of the evidence used, and to seek expert advice
when needed.

THE 18 STATEMENTS cover the four physical domains as follows:

Domain 1 — Nutrition and Hydration:
- They drink enough clean water
- They eat nutritious, palatable and sufficient food
- The food is offered in ways that are varied and interesting for their species

Domain 2 — Physical Environment:
- They can avoid unpleasant lighting levels, noises, and odours
- They can choose to occupy areas that are clean and promote physical, and thermal comfort
- They live in a place that allows them to be calm, to rest and to sleep

Domain 3 — Health and Fitness:
- They are free of injuries or signs of disease
- They are a healthy bodyweight and they have an appropriate level of fitness
- They appear to be free from acute or chronic pain, or distress

Domain 4a — Interactions with the Environment:
- They live in an interesting and stimulating place
- They have enough room to move around easily
- They can choose to exercise and explore freely

Domain 4b — Interactions with Other Animals:
- They can choose how long they spend interacting with other animals of their own species
- They have positive interactions with other animals of their own species
- They can avoid conflicts with animals of other species

Domain 4c — Interactions with Humans:
- They have healthy bonds with their carer(s)
- They interact with familiar people confidently and calmly
- They are calm in the presence of most people

When a user records a high level of confidence and a low welfare outcome, the summary
card will prompt them into action. A low level of confidence combined with a low welfare
outcome will encourage the user to find more evidence or seek expert advice.


LIMITATIONS

The Mellorater is not a welfare measuring tool. It relies on self-reporting and is
vulnerable to user subjectivity. It serves as a memory aid and attention-focusing device
that prompts users to reflect on the physical and behavioural domains of their animal's
life-world.

It does not set thresholds of what is or is not acceptable welfare. It is species-agnostic
and designed to help the user identify husbandry practices commonly associated with
positive mental states, and to highlight areas of potential welfare compromise.

There are instances where certain restrictions are imposed in an animal's best interest,
either temporarily or to mitigate other risks. Low welfare readings are to be expected in
such cases. The Notes fields allow users to record specific and local circumstances to
explain why a low welfare reading in any domain is to be expected.

The app is not designed to generate scores that can be aggregated into a single overall
welfare score. Users are advised not to assume that each domain is as equally impactful
on their animal's quality of life as the next.

APPROPRIATE USE of the Mellorater:
- For evaluating and reflecting on the animal's life-world (Umwelt) across all domains
- For identifying and resolving conditions that may give rise to negative experiences
- For improving an individual animal's lived experience incrementally

INAPPROPRIATE use of the Mellorater:
- Not for 'measuring' welfare
- Not for producing scores that can be aggregated into a single overall welfare score
- Not for providing proof of good or bad welfare state

PAPER 3: THE TELEONOME — A FRAMEWORK FOR UNDERSTANDING ANIMAL WELFARE
INTEGRATING ADAPTIVE CAPABILITIES, AFFECTIVE REGULATION, AGENCY, AND
ENVIRONMENTAL AFFORDANCES
================================================================================

THE TELEONOME DEFINED

The teleonome is the organised biological system through which organisms pursue
biological goals (telos). It comprises the integrated system of perceptual, physiological,
behavioural, and adaptive capabilities shaped by natural selection to pursue survival and
reproduction in dynamic environments. Each species, population, and ultimately each
organism possesses its own teleonome: a set of inherited traits and interconnected
regulatory processes that provide them with potential for solving problems of viability in
particular ecological contexts.

The teleonome is a relational property that specifies the organism's fit within the
structure of its environment. This fit depends on what the environment makes possible for
the organism to do — the opportunities for action it presents to beings with particular
bodies, senses, and skills. In ecological psychology these "opportunities for action" are
called affordances.


SENTIENCE, AFFORDANCES, AND AGENCY

For sentient animals, the teleonome includes affective subsystems that evaluate
environmental conditions (physical and social) and internal states in terms of biological
relevance, producing experiences of valence and urgency that guide individual adaptive
responses. These affective phenomena are themselves teleonomic — they are evolved tools
for flexible, rapid assessment and response (pain isn't just information; it is a teleonomic
alarm).

AFFORDANCES: The teleonome embodies inherited capabilities organised around
detecting, valuing, and exploiting affordances. Under artificial selection, in captive or
managed conditions, the relationships among teleonome and affordances are often
fundamentally altered. Some teleonomic systems that evolved to detect, value, and
generate affordances may be reduced in scope, bypassed, or rendered functionally
redundant, while others are selectively amplified.

AGENCY: Agency is the active expression of teleonomic organisation — the biological
impetus to act within and on the world in ways that serve teleonomic goals. Where agency
is present but constrained, welfare inferences must be extremely cautious: motivational
systems are present but obstructed. Complete absence of agency indicates regulatory
collapse — the animal gives up and stops attempting to exert control over outcomes.


ALLOSTATIC LOAD AND WELFARE TRAJECTORIES

Allostatic load is the accumulating physiological and psychological costs of maintaining
viability. When challenges are excessive, persistent, and/or unpredictable, the cumulative
effort to restore stability can constrain future responsiveness and reduce resilience.

Teleonomic systems keep adjusting until they are no longer able to; therefore, endurance
in suboptimal conditions should not be misinterpreted as resilience. Animals may continue
to function while accruing cumulative costs, and it is precisely the organism's persistence
in adapting that can systematically hide deteriorating welfare trajectories.


HIERARCHIES OF AFFECTIVE CONCERN

Affective processes are organised hierarchically from immediate, survival-related concerns
to broader, long-term ones. Proximal concerns (breathlessness, pain, hunger, thirst,
thermoregulatory perception) signal immediate threats to physiological stability. Distal
concerns operate over longer timeframes, shaping future behaviour, learning and mood.
The layers interact such that chronic disruption of distal concerns can eventually alter
physiological regulation.

The animal affectome encompasses both self-regulatory, interoceptive systems (e.g.,
breathlessness, pain, hunger, thermoregulation) and social affective systems that monitor
and regulate relationships with conspecifics (e.g., isolation distress, affiliative bonding,
social confidence). For social species, both interoceptive and social-affective systems are
equally fundamental to maintaining viability. Welfare assessments that prioritise
physiological indicators while treating social deprivation as secondary fail to recognise
the integrated teleonomic architecture through which these animals regulate their existence.


COMMON INTERPRETIVE ERRORS TO AVOID

A teleonomic framework can help avoid common interpretive errors, including:
- Misclassifying high arousal as preference
- Mistaking decreases in physiological measures for calm states
- Assuming that no measurable change implies no welfare effect
- Construing behavioural inhibition due to an animal's low expectations of control as
  indicating resolution
- Interpreting restrained or controlled behaviour as evidence of assent, especially during
  interactions with humans
